Lavande · Retail

How do you turn a retail space into a sensory bubble, a way to escape the bustle of the city?

Retail · Concept store · 2023

Lavande

A concept store entirely devoted to lavender, but not a shop you simply pass through. A place where you linger: you pick your flowers in the garden, arrange them in an all-purple workshop, and settle in for a tea on the terrace.

The place

An interior opening onto two gardens: a flower garden in front of the entrance, a terrace right beside it.

Inside, the shop takes up the heart of the place. The café and its counter run along the windows, and the workshop sits at the back, sheltered.

In front of the entrance

The picking garden

Even before opening the door, you cross a garden: a winding path slips between the flower beds, and your pace slows down on its own.

Those who have booked a flower-arranging workshop are welcomed here. They pick the flowers that inspire them, the raw material of their future bouquet.

At the heart of the place

The shop

Here, you are in Provence. The display units borrow the silhouette of the local farmhouses: golden stone, gabled roofs, arched windows where lavender blooms.

The furniture traces gentle aisles, in tones that recall the South. On the shelves, Provençal products all linked to lavender: homeware, fine food, beauty.

At the back

The workshop

Flowers picked, you head to the workshop, at the back, set apart. The room is designed as a bubble outside time: as if you were inside the lavender itself.

Purple lines the walls and wraps around you, the scent of the flowers fills the room, the outside world fades away. In the centre stands the arranging table, where everyone puts together their bouquet, guided step by step.

On the floor, a colourful terrazzo recalls the petals that fall during the workshops.

The café side

The counter

On the café side, you order an ice cream, a lavender-scented tea, a sweet treat from Provence.

The counter is curved, to bring softness into the space. Behind it, a textured glass partition adds modernity and depth, and catches the light.

Along the windows

The café

In the café, you drift towards the French Riviera. The benches are inspired by the design of Riviera boats, and the yellow and wood tones recall afternoons by the water.

Outside

The terrace

Right next to the garden, the terrace extends the café into the sun. Yellow chairs, round tables and lavender planters: you linger there, an ice cream or a tea in hand.

You leave with a bouquet made with your own hands, and the scent of lavender lingering a little longer.
