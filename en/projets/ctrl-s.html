How do you give form to the intangible: designing a memorial space for our digital archives?

Discover the project
0memories indexed

What becomes of our digital traces when we are gone? CTRL+S gives them an address deep in the quarries of Les Baux: you sort through your life, engrave it in glass, and those who remain come to find it again.

  • Select: choose the memories that truly matter
  • Purge: erase the superfluous, in a single gesture
  • Archive: engrave the essential in glass, deep in the stone
  • Consult: find again the memory of a loved one
  • Breathe: come back to light and life

Inside the stone of Les Baux

The quarries of Les Baux-de-Provence: limestone naves 25 metres high, where daylight barely reaches. A place outside time, equal to the question: what remains of us?

Act I, the depositor

During their lifetime, the depositor sorts through their digital memory: they choose what to pass on and let the rest go.

Select

The selection room

Walking through one's memories

The walls are screens: thousands of images and messages all around. The depositor walks through their memories and keeps the ones that matter.

Purge

The purge room

A single button

One last time, their memories play across the quarry's 25 metres. Then a single button: the superfluous disappears, the essential will be passed on.

The urns

What is kept is engraved in a glass plate, slipped into a capsule: the urn. A technology inspired by Microsoft's Project Silica.

Act II, the visitors

Later, loved ones walk through the archives, then settle in to consult the memories of the one who has gone.

Archive

The archive corridor

A constellation of urns

The urns cover tall panels rising the full height of the quarries: visitors walk through this corridor beneath a constellation of memories.

Consult

The consultation rooms

At the heart of memories

First an airlock, then the circular room where you gather around the urn: a path that slowly draws you into the heart of memories.

On entering, your eye is caught by a beam of light striking the urn reader's table: this is where the urn is placed.

The light passes through the glass, the device reads its content, then goes out to make way for the projection.

Breathe

The garden café

Back to the light

At the end of the path, a garden café brings back light and life: visitors catch their breath before leaving.

Student project led by Camille, Matthias and Pauline, studio « sésame ».
