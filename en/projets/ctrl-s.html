How do you give form to the intangible: designing a memorial space for our digital archives?

Scenography · Carrières des Baux-de-Provence, France · 2026

CTRL+S

Thousands of photos, messages, traces: what becomes of them when we are gone? CTRL+S gives them an address, deep in the quarries of Les Baux. Here you sort through your digital life, engrave it in glass — and those who remain come down into the stone to find it again.

  • Select — choose the memories that truly matter
  • Purge — erase the superfluous, in a single gesture
  • Archive — engrave the essential in glass, deep in the stone
  • Consult — find again the memory of a loved one
  • Breathe — come back to light and life

Inside the stone of Les Baux

The project settles in the quarries of Les Baux-de-Provence: limestone naves 25 metres high, where daylight barely reaches.

A place outside time, on the scale of the question it asks — what remains of us.

Notebook sketches — the consultation room, the sphere, the device that reads the urns.

Before the stone — research & sketches

Act I — The depositor

While still alive, the depositor comes to sort through their digital memory. They choose what to pass on — to loved ones, to descendants, to the world — and let the rest go.

Select

The selection room

Walking through one's memories

The walls are interactive screens: thousands of images, videos and messages unfolding all around. The depositor literally walks through their memories and keeps the ones that matter.

Purge

The purge room

A single button

One last time, they see all their memories, projected over the full height of the quarry — 25 metres of images.

Then they press a single button: the superfluous data disappears, only the essential will be passed on.

The urns

What is kept is engraved in a glass plate, slipped into a capsule: the urn. A technology inspired by Microsoft's Project Silica, so that memory can outlast time.

Act II — The visitors

Later, loved ones come in turn. They pass through the archives, then settle in to consult the memories of the person who is gone.

Archive

The archive corridor

A constellation of urns

The urns hang on large panels that rise to the full height of the quarries. Visitors walk through this giant corridor, beneath a constellation of memories, to the consultation rooms.

Consult

The consultation rooms

At the heart of memories

Every consultation room is designed the same way: an immersive path that gradually draws you into the heart of the memories.

You first wait in an airlock, before entering the circular room where you gather around the urn.

Breathe

The garden café

Back to the light

At the end of the journey, a garden café brings back light and life. The urns stay in the stone; visitors catch their breath before leaving.

Student project led by a team of three — Camille, Matthias and Pauline, studio « sésame ».
